Can a policy learn the chain rule — not just one chain length?

We repeatedly solve integer chains x₀ > x₁ > … > xₙ₋₁. An exact dynamic program supplies training labels and the ground truth optimum. A bidirectional recurrent policy trains across a range of short lengths, then we test whether it extrapolates to much longer chains than it has ever seen.

What changed from the first version

Algorithmic-generalization test

The old CNN was trained at one fixed length and had a finite receptive field, so strong performance at exactly that length could be memorization. This version samples multiple training lengths and deliberately benchmarks lengths up to roughly 3× the training maximum.

The explicit chain-length feature has been removed. Inputs are only the target value and relative position.

Recurrent policy

A bidirectional GRU reads the sequence from both directions and reuses the same recurrent transition at every position. That makes evaluation at unseen lengths structurally natural instead of relying on a fixed convolutional receptive field.

This remains imitation learning from the exact DP oracle. The purpose is still PoC A: test whether a reusable neural heuristic can represent and extrapolate the structural rule before introducing RL or self-improvement.

Everything runs locally in your browser. No training data is uploaded.
